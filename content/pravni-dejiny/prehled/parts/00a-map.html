Foundations

1 Introduction: methods, sources, aims: reconstruct past law by the standards of its own time; four kinds of legal source; read every extract critically.

How to use this PDF: the exam page and reading list → the timeline and the Austria line → 24 topics → the connections page at the end.

Antiquity & religious legal cultures

2 Ancient cultures, Islamic law: writing in the Fertile Crescent (Hammurabi); Rome invents legal scholarship → Corpus Iuris Civilis 529/534; fiqh = jurists' law (622 Hijra).

5 Jewish legal history: personal, not territorial law; no state for about 2,000 years; "governed by men, not rules"; in the Empire a particular law that even Christian courts applied.

Middle Ages & early modern

3 Middle Ages: fragmented law (Germanic codes, feudal and town law, Sachsenspiegel c. 1225) under one learned law, Roman + canon, taught at universities (Digest found c. 1155).

4 Ius commune: reception through academically trained courts, not by decree (1495 Imperial Chamber Court); humanism and the usus modernus.

6 Common law: royal writs and juries; no reception; re-invented as "ancient custom" against the king. A sibling of the ius commune (Herzog). 1066 · 1215.

17th–18th century

7 Westphalian order: the religious wars make the modern state; 1648 gives power to the princes; Bodin's sovereignty; absolutism.

8 Natural law, Enlightenment, revolution: Salamanca and Grotius free natural law from God → the law of reason → codes (1794/1804/1811); individual rights and the revolutions (1776, 1789).

19th century — codes, constitutions, empires

91011 ⇄ Constitutionalism & norm production: three models (code / judge / professor: France, England, Germany); Savigny vs Thibaut 1814; Austria 1848 → 1867; rights on paper vs in court.

12 Legal education & professions: England by apprenticeship, Prussia through the state, Austria a mix (Thun 1852/55).

13 Imperialism: colonies and extraterritoriality; Berlin 1884–85 (effective occupation).

14 Chinese legal history: one penal code per dynasty; Confucian ideology + Legalist punishment; reform 1902–11 to end extraterritoriality.

1914–1945 — war, republic, dictatorship

15 WWI and Paris: the empires collapse; from "mutual oblivion" to war guilt; League of Nations; St-Germain Art 88 forbids union with Germany.

1617 ⇄ First Republic, B-VG: democracy as a legal form; Kelsen's B-VG 1920 and the first constitutional court; 1929 amendment; end in 1933/34.

1819 ⇄ Dictatorships: power seized in legal form (Enabling Act 1933); the dual state; the Ständestaat and the Anschluss; Nuremberg → international criminal law → ICC.

After 1945 — Europe and the world

20 Cold War 1945–55: Austria from Allied control to the State Treaty and neutrality; the blocs; ECSC.

21 European integration: Rome 1957 → Maastricht 1992 → Lisbon 2007; Austria joins 1995.

22 Her Story: codes promise equality but subordinate the wife; suffrage and the professions; partnership marriage 1975.

2324 ⇄ Globalisation of law: UN, human rights, decolonisation; GATT/WTO; Kennedy's three globalizations.
