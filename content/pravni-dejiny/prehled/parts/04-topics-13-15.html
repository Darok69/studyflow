The protectorate (Art 34 puts it beside seizure)

The weaker entity keeps its own ruler but hands the stronger state its foreign relations, usually its defence, often key administration. Examples: Tunisia 1881 and Morocco 1912 (France), Egypt 1914 (Britain), German Schutzgebiete.

Why? Cheaper and more palatable than annexation: no full administration, less irritation of rivals, no duty to grant citizenship, sold at home as protection, not conquest. Basis: a treaty of protection = the ruler's "consent", given under threat, often without understanding the text.

Why European codes spread outside Europe

  • Colonial imposition (directly ruled lands)
  • Getting rid of the capitulations: Japan, Ottoman Empire, Egypt, Siam

Armistices and after

  • Villa Giusti (3 Nov 1918): the monarchy had already dissolved; based on the Fourteen Points.
  • Mudros (30 Oct 1918): with Britain only; no Fourteen Points → the Allies bound by nothing.
  • Later: US–Austrian peace 1921; Lausanne 1923 replaces Sèvres, ends the capitulations.

Brest vs Paris: why they hardly compare

  • Brest was dictated by the Central Powers, Paris by the Allies: same war, opposite victors.
  • Brest = annexation under a self-determination label; Paris = framed by self-determination. Principles depended on who won.
  • Brest: Russia treated as an equal, joint dinners ("revolutionary tactics crossed swords with imperial cabinet diplomacy"). Paris: the Austrians kept in a separate quarter, "more like prisoners".
  • Trotsky's "no war, no peace" → Germany's Operation Faustschlag (17 Feb 1918) → signed under protest.
  • Paris preambles: war "arose from hostilities begun by" the loser; Brest art 1: the war has simply ended.
  • Not admitted to Paris: Austria-Hungary, the Ottomans; Russia ignored.

Sèvres: two years and a vanished partner

  • Interests "too diverse to be managed"; Ottomans demand the 1878 borders (Jun 1919) → talks postponed Jul–Nov 1919.
  • Mustafa Kemal defies the sultan (May 1919), Erzurum, Ankara assembly 23 Apr 1920.
  • Constantinople occupied 5 Mar 1920 as a "hostage". Foch: enforcement needs 325,000 men.
  • San Remo (Apr 1920): mandates Syria → France, Palestine + Mesopotamia → Britain; Kurds only autonomy; Armenia left alone.
  • The option rules foreshadow the population exchange after Lausanne 1923.
The Treaty of Sèvres was as fragile as the porcelain of that name, alas without its charm.Philip Marshall Brown
What to say: signed "with a contracting party that in reality no longer existed" (Gassner).

The name German-Austria: distance from the monarchy + belonging to the German nation. New states of the war: Czechoslovakia, Poland, Finland, Yugoslavia; also Austria, Hungary, the Baltic states.

Old state or new?

Discontinuity thesis (Kelsen, opinion of 29 Nov 1918): German-Austria is a new state, not the monarchy's legal successor. Accepted only by Britain; France: Austria + Hungary are clear successors.

Formal discontinuity, material continuity

The form breaks: empire → republic, monarch deposed, new assembly. The substance stays: same officials, courts, administration, ABGB.

All sovereign rights and other privileges of the House of Habsburg-Lorraine … are abolished in Austria for all time.Habsburg Law, § 1(1)
What to say: three steps: abolition (§ 1) · expulsion (§ 2), unless members renounce membership and claims and declare loyalty · confiscation: the Republic owns imperial property (§ 5).

Saint-Germain, seen from Vienna

  • Renner's delegation summoned only in May 1919; draft 2 June; Otto Bauer (pro-Anschluss) resigns; 6 Aug reply: financial clauses now reflect the dissolution, no confiscation of German-Austrian property; guilt (art 177) stays.
  • Nationality by Heimatrecht (inherited municipal belonging, 1849–1939), not residence.
  • Only defeated state to gain land: Burgenland (Allies hoped for help against Hungarian communism).
  • France cared only to weaken Germany → Anschluss ban, name change to "Austria", all Czechoslovak claims backed; Italy: South Tyrol.
Austria's independence is irrevocable unless the Council of the League of Nations agrees to a change.St Germain, Art 88 (as rendered in the lecture)
What to say: the "heart" of the political clauses; Austria must avoid anything endangering its independence. Paradox: self-determination for the successor nations, a ban on using it for German Austria → Topics 16–19

The League of Nations and what was new in 1919

  • Covenant = Part I (arts 1–26) of every Paris treaty (Wilson's demand); for that reason the US did not ratify St Germain. Geneva, 1920–1946; Assembly, Council, Secretariat → model for the UN.
  • Aims: collective security, disarmament (art 8), peaceful settlement (arts 8–17), prohibition of war (art 15), minorities, labour. ILO: "world peace can only be built on social justice".
  1. a permanent organisation: from conferences to institutions
  2. limits on ius ad bellum: compulsory settlement, 3-month wait, sanctions
  3. self-determination as organising principle
  4. minority protection under the League (model: Poland, 28 Jun 1919)
  5. mandates (art 22): a colony as a "sacred trust", not property
  6. Permanent Court of International Justice, ILO
  7. art 227: first try at a head of state's criminal responsibility
