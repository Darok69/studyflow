The one-sentence story

You possess by animus + corpus; with a iusta causa from the owner you become owner by traditio; from a non-owner you usucapt in 1 or 2 years, protected meanwhile by the publiciana; once owner you use the rei vindicatio, and you can burden the thing with servitudes and pledges.

The three classic tensions

  • owner vs bona fide acquirer: lex Atinia, publiciana, exceptio iusti dominii
  • material vs labour: accessio, specificatio, building
  • creditor's security vs owner's autonomy: convalescence (Paulus vs Modestinus), D 20.1.29.2

Glossary

Latin maxims and phrases — by topic

quote them exactly in your exegesis

Persons & possession

  • nasciturus pro iam nato habetur, quotiens de commodis eius agitur — the unborn child counts as born when it benefits
  • si quid universitati debetur, singulis non debetur — a corporation is not its members (D 3.4.7.1)
  • animo et corpore — the two elements of possession
  • oculis et affectu — possession without touching (D 41.2.1.21)
  • nec vi nec clam nec precario — not by force, not secretly, not by revocable grant
  • vim fieri veto / restituas — prohibitory / restitutory interdict
  • vim vi repellere licet — force may be met with force
  • nemo sibi ipse causam possessionis mutare potest — no self-made title
  • animo nostro, corpore alieno — our intent, another's body (D 41.2.3.12)
  • quia multa accidere possunt, ut eam non capiamus — the wounded animal (D 41.1.5.1)

Ownership & transfer

  • nihil commune habet proprietas cum possessione — D 41.2.12.1
  • nemo plus iuris transferre potest quam ipse habet — D 41.1.20 pr
  • hunc ego hominem ex iure Quiritium meum esse aio… — the mancipatio formula
  • per traditionem quoque iure naturali res nobis adquiruntur — Inst 2.1.40
  • venire contra factum proprium — going against one's own earlier conduct
  • benignius est — the kinder solution (D 39.5.25)

Usucapio

  • res habilis, titulus, bona fides, possessio, tempus
  • mala fides superveniens non nocet — later bad faith does no harm
  • tolerabilis error — an excusable error (putative title)

Natural modes

  • accessio cedit principali — the addition follows the main thing
  • superficies solo cedit — what is on the land follows the land
  • sine materia nulla species effici possit — Sabinians
  • quod factum est, antea nullius fuerat — Proculians
  • neque malitiis indulgendum est — malice gets no protection (D 6.1.38)

Protection

  • ubi rem meam invenio, ibi vindico — where I find my thing, I claim it
  • omnis condemnatio pecuniaria est — every condemnation is in money
  • si paret rem … ex iure Quiritium Auli Agerii esse — intentio
  • neque ea res restituetur · quanti ea res erit
  • pro possessione dolus est — fraud takes the place of possession
  • si ea res Numerii Negidii non sit — exceptio iusti dominii

Servitudes

  • servitus in faciendo consistere nequit — no duty to act
  • ususfructus est ius alienis rebus utendi fruendi salva rerum substantia — D 7.1.1
  • nulli res sua servit — no servitude over your own thing
  • servitus civiliter exercenda est — use it considerately

Pledge

  • prior tempore, potior iure — earlier in time, stronger in law
  • indivisa est pignoris causa — the pledge lasts until the debt is paid in full
  • melior est causa possidentis — equal rank: the pledgee in possession is stronger
