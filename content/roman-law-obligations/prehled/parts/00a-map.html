An obligation binds one person (debtor) to another (creditor). It is a right in personam, enforced against one person only, unlike the property rights in rem. Read the map from the source of the obligation (left) to what can go wrong and who pays (right). Pills point to the chapters.

Solving a case — always ask

who wants what from whom → on which basis (contract, enrichment, delict) → which action → requirements → defences → result

Liability standards — one scale

dolus → culpa lata → culpa → custodia → vis maior (nobody is liable). Utility principle: whoever gets the benefit of the contract answers more strictly. Ch. II · III

Two actions per contract

depositum, commodatum, pignus, mandatum: an actio directa (main claim) plus an actio contraria (counter-claims: expenses, damage). mutuum: the condictio. Sale, hire, partnership: one action for each side (empti/venditi, locati/conducti, pro socio).

Damages — what is owed?

reliance interest (as if the contract had never been made) vs expectation interest (as if it had been performed); damnum emergens + lucrum cessans. Ch. IV · V · XV
