Where property law meets the law of obligations

most exam traps sit on this border
left = property PDF · right = this PDF

Almost every case asks two questions at once: who owns or possesses the thing? (property) and who owes what to whom? (obligations). These are the places where the two meet. Read the left column first if property law is still new to you.

Sale ≠ transferOwnership passes only by a conveyance: traditio with iusta causa, or mancipatio for res mancipi.The sale only creates obligations: the seller must deliver, the buyer must pay. The buyer is a creditor, not yet owner.Prop VII · Obl II, IV
Loan for consumptionThe borrower becomes owner of the money (traditio); mixed or spent coins: nummos consumere.mutuum is a real contract; the lender has only the condictio for the same amount.Prop VII, IX · Obl III
Holding for anotherDepositary, borrower for use, lessee = detentors, not possessors: the owner keeps possession through them.depositum, commodatum, locatio conductio: duties of custody and return; who bears the risk of loss.Prop II · Obl III, VII

Every case: two lenses

  • Property lens: who is owner or possessor of each thing now? Did a conveyance happen (and was it valid)?
  • Obligations lens: which contract, enrichment or delict links the parties? Which action, which liability standard, which defence?
  • Then combine: vindicate if you still own, sue on the contract if you have one, condict if ownership passed without a reason.

Three words that mean different things

  • custodia: in property the test for keeping possession; in obligations a strict liability standard.
  • specification of goods in a sale (ascertaining which item is sold) ≠ specificatio (making a new thing).
  • pignus: the pledged thing, the right in rem, and the real contract.
